# Neural Opponents: Review After Stage 4

*Written 2026-11-27. Everything here was played in a cloud container on CPU, on `jpfeiff-updates` at `63a22e1`, with the net `runs/capacity/small/latest.pt` (iteration 1870, 0.18M parameters).*

This is an independent review of the [neural opponent sprint](neural-opponents-sprint.md) after its Stage 4: how the best net plays, how that compares with strong human play on this patch, whether the direction still makes sense, and what to do next. The next steps were approved on 2026-11-26 and are [Stage 6](neural-opponents-sprint.md#stage-6-teach-the-late-game) of the sprint. The scripts behind the numbers are in [`rl/scripts/review/`](../rl/scripts/review/README.md).

## Verdict

The best net wins its ladder by sitting on Tavern Tier 2 and 3 and out-tempoing the lobby. Strong players win this patch on Tier 5 and 6. The sprint built a sound trainer and a sound ladder, and the net has settled into a strategy that the ladder cannot see as weak.

- **Leveling curve: no.** On turn 11 its seats average Tier 3.1, and 3% of them are on Tier 5 or higher. Strong players are on Tier 4 by turn 7 and Tier 6 by about turn 11. Search does not change this.
- **Strong comps: one, at about a tenth of human output.** It found the Mech magnetize comp by itself, and a Mech board wins 103 of the 106 lobbies that have Mechs. It never builds the Tier 5 and 6 engines behind this patch's top comps, and it ignores seven of the ten minion types.
- **Absolute strength: far below human.** Its boards on turns 12 to 15 win 1% to 5% of fights against 13 published human winner boards from the same turns. They break even only when the human stats are cut to about 5%.
- **The endgame theory: right, with one correction.** The early and mid game is not noise. It is a well-tuned answer to a late game that nobody in the training pool plays. Forcing the net onto the human leveling curve costs it 0.57 places, so more self-play of the same kind will not move it.
- **Direction: keep the goal and the tools, change the next stage.** Add an absolute human yardstick first. Then teach the late game before anything else. Park search tuning and bigger nets until that is done.
- **Clock.** The engine models Patch 37.0.2. Season 15 goes live on December 1 and removes Trinkets and Dark Gifts. Half of the Tier 5 and 6 minions on the net's boards come from Dark Gifts.

## How strong players play this patch

Strong players level on a fixed early curve, save their Gold for Tavern Tiers before rolls, and win with engines that live on Tier 5 and 6.

- **Leveling.** The standard curve is Tier 2 on turn 2, Tier 3 on turn 5 and Tier 4 on turn 6 or 7 ([esports.gg leveling guide](https://esports.gg/guides/hearthstone/hearthstone-battlegrounds-leveling-guide/)). The guides I could open stop there. Tier 5 around turn 9 and Tier 6 around turn 11 is common practice and my own estimate, not a sourced figure.
- **Rolling.** The same guide says to avoid rolling during the first five turns. [CarryPotter's tips](https://esports.gg/guides/hearthstone/100-tips-for-hearthstone-battlegrounds-by-carrypotter) say to roll as little as possible in the first six turns and count a freeze as a lost Gold.
- **Triples.** Triples are timed to the level, so the Discover comes from a higher tier. CarryPotter's benchmark is a Tier 5 minion from a triple on turn 6 and a Tier 6 minion on turn 8.
- **Dark Gifts.** The button costs 3 Gold, opens on turn 3, and works once a turn and three times a game. The minion's tier follows the turn, not the Tavern Tier: Tier 5 enters on turn 8 and Tier 6 on turn 9 ([IDC](https://idcgames.com/en/hearthstone/news/hearthstone-battlegrounds-season-14-introduces-dark-gifts), [Amalgadon](https://www.amalgadon.com/dark-gifts)).
- **Balance.** The [37.0.2 notes](https://hsbg.cards/patch-notes/37.0.2) call Quilboar and Demons the stronger types and nerf Living Azerite.

The comps that win are engine comps. This is [hspulse.net's November 2026 list](https://hspulse.net/bg/comps), top two tiers, with each core card's Tavern Tier from the engine's catalog.

## Leveling

The curve does not make sense against strong opponents. From turn 7 on, the net is two to three Tavern Tiers behind strong players, with or without search.

- **When it levels.** Among seats that get there, the median first turn on Tier 3 is 7 and on Tier 4 is 12. Only 59% of seats ever reach Tier 4, 28% Tier 5 and 13% Tier 6.
- **Search changes nothing here.** Inside the same lobbies, search seats and plain seats level alike: Tier 3.0 against 2.9 on turn 9, and 3.6 against 3.4 on turn 12. When all eight seats search, the curve is lower still.
- **It waits for discounts.** 23% of its Tavern upgrades are bought for 0 or 1 Gold. With search that is 34%.
- **It rolls instead.** Rolls a turn go from 1 on turn 6 to 3.3 on turn 8 and 5.6 on turn 15, and it ends turns with 0.3 Gold or less.
- **Leveling alone is not the answer.** 9kgamerbot follows the human curve and has the weakest boards in the pool. The net places 1.7 on a ladder full of such bots.
- **The from-scratch run starts even slower.** At iteration 750 it is still on Tier 1 through turn 3 and averages Tier 2.3 on turn 9.

## Two what-ifs

Forcing the net to level like a human makes it worse, and the faster the curve the bigger the loss. That is the strongest sign that it sits in a local optimum.

**Forced leveling.** Four seats are the net with one change: whenever the Tavern upgrade is legal and the seat is below the curve's Tier for that turn, the upgrade is taken. Four seats are the plain net. Each row is 200 lobbies, and 4.5 is level.

| Forced curve (Tier on turn) | Forced seats' average place | Places lost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fast: 2 on turn 2, 3 on 4, 4 on 6, 5 on 8, 6 on 10 | 5.36 ± 0.06 | 0.86 |
| Standard: 2 on turn 2, 3 on 5, 4 on 7, 5 on 9, 6 on 11 | 5.07 ± 0.06 | 0.57 |
| Slow: 2 on turn 2, 3 on 5, 4 on 8, 5 on 10, 6 on 12 | 4.84 ± 0.06 | 0.34 |
| To Tier 4 only: 2 on turn 2, 3 on 5, 4 on 7 | 4.69 ± 0.07 | 0.19 |
| Late only: own play to turn 8, then 4 on turn 9, 5 on 11, 6 on 13 | 4.61 ± 0.07 | 0.11 |

- **The cost is tempo that never comes back.** On the standard curve the forced seats have 1,166 total stats on turn 12 against 1,393 for the plain seats, and 15 Health against 22. They pass the plain seats only on turn 16.
- **Tier 6 buys them almost nothing.** They are on Tier 6 from turn 11 and hold 2.4 Tier 5 and 6 minions on turn 12 against 1.5. Against the human boards they do no better than the plain net.
- **Late levels do not help either.** The last row leaves the early game alone and still ends slightly below level.

**Greedy play.** Four seats always take the policy's top action and four sample as trained, over 400 lobbies. The greedy seats place 4.42 ± 0.04 against 4.58. Half temperature gives the same 4.42. Search on this net places 4.13 against 4.87, so about a fifth of the search's gain is removed randomness and the rest is real lookahead inside the turn.

What a strong player would question in these games:

- **The lobby copies itself.** In 9200004 all eight seats ended on Mechs. In 9200002 six seats built Pirates, one built Demons, and nobody built Beasts, Elementals or Quilboar. Strong players read contested types and move off them.

- **Late turns are all rolls.** Seats roll 3 to 6 times a turn from turn 9, looking for copies of Tier 1 to 3 cards. Strong players spend those turns on Tier 5 and 6 shops.

## Why self-play settled here

The net sits at a stable point. Every single step toward the human way of playing loses places, and nothing in its training pool punishes staying put.

1. **Leveling has a negative return for this policy.** The forced-leveling test measures it. Forced seats fall behind by turn 12 (1,166 total stats against 1,393, and 15 Health against 22) and only draw level on turn 16.
2. **The payoff is several steps away.** A scaling engine needs a level, two or three specific cards, a few turns of holding them, and then the loop itself. Each step alone costs tempo. PPO explores one sampled action at a time, so it almost never strings the chain together and sees it pay.
3. **Nobody in the pool shows it a scaling board.** Self-play copies, older snapshots and the rule bots all peak low. 9kgamerbot levels like a human and has the weakest boards of all: a median of 527 total stats on turn 12 against the net's 1,421. The net has learned that leveling loses, and in this pool that is true.
4. **The critic and the search share the blind spot.** Search rolls the same policy to the end of the turn and scores with the same critic. It finds better buys inside a turn. It cannot value a Tavern Tier that the critic has never seen pay off.
5. **Two limits cap the engines it could find.** A turn is force-ended after 30 actions, and that cap already binds in 5% to 8% of turns 8 to 12. The top human comps this patch are high-action comps. Board order is also outside the policy (`--no-reposition`) and the position solver is off, since it measured no gain at this level.
6. **The drift was visible, but no gate looked at it.** On the fast ladder the net's mean Tier at turn 8 was 3.8 at iteration 150, 3.0 at iteration 600, and is 2.8 now. Every ladder gate still passed, because a ladder only ranks agents against each other.

The from-scratch `--pure` run is heading to the same place. At iteration 750 it is on Tier 3.1 at turn 12 and scores 2.03 on the fast ladder. So this is where the method goes, not a leftover of the 9kgamerbot warm start.

## The endgame theory, weighed

The theory says strong comps are the chess endgame, and until the net finds them its early and mid game is noise. The evidence supports the first half strongly and corrects the second.

**What holds.**

- **The net has not found the endgame.** Its turn-13 boards are about a twentieth of human turn-13 boards, and it has no engine from Tier 5 or 6.
- **Earlier decisions take their value from the endgame.** Leveling on turns 5 to 9 pays only if Tier 5 and 6 are converted on turns 10 to 14. The net cannot convert them, so it learned not to level, and it was right to.
- **The fork study describes this low-tier world.** It found a game 78% settled by turn 10. That is true when nobody scales. In human lobbies the scaling turns decide a large share.

**What needs correcting.**

- **It is not noise.** The early game is consistent and locally well tuned: buy stats, stay low, roll hard from turn 8, spend all three Dark Gifts by turn 8. Forcing a different curve loses places, and greedy play gains almost nothing.
- **It is the answer to a different question.** It solves "how do I beat seven low-tier boards", so the split of Gold between board and Tavern Tier and the choice of comp will not survive a field that scales.
- **Some of it should carry over.** Tempo buying, triple-making and Dark Gift timing are real skills. The trainer also relearns quickly: the from-scratch run is within 0.3 places of the best net after 750 iterations.

**Where the chess analogy bends.** In chess every game reaches an endgame, so a learner sees endgames from its first day and value flows backward from them. In Battlegrounds the strong endgame is reached only by paying for it during the mid game. A learner that never pays never sees it. That makes this an exploration problem. More capacity, more search and more of the same self-play do not address it. Putting the learner into the late game directly does.

## Sprint progress, stage by stage

Stages 1 to 3 delivered what they promised. Stage 4 found a real but tactical gain. The gates were all relative, which is why the strategic problem passed through them.

| --- | --- | --- |

Two things in the plan's own reasoning deserve a second look.

- **"Search pays when the model of the world is exact."** That is why search stops at the turn. It also means search can never fix a multi-turn mistake such as not leveling. Only the learned value can, and the learned value has never seen the late game.
- **"At this level of play, board order is not where the places are."** True at Tier 2 with stat piles. It should be re-tested on boards with Taunts, cleaves and Deathrattle engines.

## Next steps, queued

The next stage should make the late game learnable and measure it against humans. Each step below has a gate, in the sprint's own style.

1. **Before December 1: save Season 14 human games.** Ask Max to keep his `Power.log` files from this season. The `replay/` crate already rebuilds a game from them turn by turn. After the patch nobody can produce new human games on these rules.
2. **Put a human yardstick on the ladder.** About two days, no training.
   - Fight the agent's end-of-turn boards against a fixed set of human boards by turn. Report the win rate at turns 10, 12 and 14. A prototype exists from this review, using the 13 published boards.
   - Replace the published boards with boards from Max's logs once they exist. Thirty games also give his real Tier-by-turn curve.
   - Print style lines in every ladder report: Tier by turn, share of Tier 5 and 6 buys, minion types played.
   - **Gate for everything after:** the turn-13 win rate against the human set, 3% today. A stage that does not move it has not moved the endgame.
3. **Endgame school: the direct test of the theory.** One to two days of Mac time.
   - Train lobbies in which every seat is put on the standard leveling curve by the environment. The cost is the same for all eight seats, so nobody is punished for it. The forced upgrade is an environment event and stays out of the PPO batch.
   - Continue from the current net for 300 to 500 iterations. Raise the action cap for these lobbies (step 5).
   - Watch the gate from step 2, the share of Tier 5 and 6 buys on Tier 6 (34% today), and Brann, Titus and Drakkari on final boards (under 1% today).
   - **If boards grow several-fold and engines appear,** exploration was the block. Go to step 4.
   - **If nothing changes with the whole lobby on Tier 6,** the block is structural: the action cap, the card representation, or credit across five or more turns. Do step 5 and add an auxiliary head that predicts the board's strength a few turns ahead, then repeat.
4. **Walk it back and keep an exploiter.** Three to five days.
   - Remove the forcing in stages: all seats, half the seats, none. Start lobbies from saved turn-8 states if episodes need to be shorter.
   - **Gate:** with no forcing, the net still levels (mean Tier 4.5 or more at turn 10), holds its human-set number, and beats today's best net head to head.
   - Keep the endgame-school net in the opponent pool at 15% to 25% of seats, so the main net keeps meeting scaling boards.
   - If it slides back to Tier 2 and still wins, the engine's damage and tempo balance is the next suspect. Compare lobby length and damage per turn with Max's logs. The net's lobbies last 17.4 turns, and the published human winners finish in 12 to 15.
5. **Lift the two caps.** Small, in parallel with step 3.
   - Raise `--max-steps-per-turn` from 30 toward the engine's own turn timer and measure the throughput cost.
   - Re-test `--auto-position` on endgame-school boards.
6. **Then return to search and distillation.** A critic that has seen engines pay has something for the search to find and for distillation to pass on. Until then, freeze search at its current default.
7. **Patch decision for James and Max.** Season 15 removes Trinkets and Dark Gifts on December 1. I would finish steps 1 to 4 on 37.0.2, since a method that finds engines on one patch should find them on the next. Track hours from scratch to the gate as the cost of a new patch.

**Not worth more time now:** more iterations of the current recipe (500 iterations moved each net 0.04 to 0.07 places in the capacity test), a bigger net, more search knobs, or a longer search horizon scored by the same critic.

## Caveats

The size of the gap is safe. Its exact number is not.

- **The human boards are showcase winners.** There is one per comp, from games the page lists at about 7,300 to 15,500 points, which I read as rating. They overstate a typical top-four board. The page rounds stats ("1K") and omits keywords, trinkets and Hero Powers, which understates them.
- **No human game was run through the engine.** Nothing here proves the engine reaches a 50,000-stat board from a real shop sequence. The net's own best board reached 27,000 total stats by turn 17, so heavy scaling does exist in it. Step 2 of the queue closes this.
- **The strong-player Tier curve after turn 7 is my estimate.** Max's logs would replace it.
- **I could not search Reddit from this environment.** Community views here come through guide and stats sites.
- **Samples.** Plain self-play and each what-if have 200 lobbies or more. The all-search and slow-ladder sets are small and are for reading games, not for rating. The sprint's own 400-lobby runs remain the ratings.
- **The forced-leveling test measures this net, not the game.** It forces only the upgrade. The net then shops on Tiers it rarely saw in training.
- **One checkpoint.** Everything played here is iteration 1870. The `--pure` run's ladder file shows the same style, and I did not play it.
- **Two cards from the human lists are not in the engine's catalog:** Magicfin Apprentice and Tide Oracle Morgl.
